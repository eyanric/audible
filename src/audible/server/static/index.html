What the numbers mean

The four columns

Rank
Where he sits when every player is put in order by the points we expect him to score under this league's own scoring — which pays a receiver half a point a catch and a running back nothing. Ja'Marr Chase shows 25: twenty-four players are expected to outscore him. Start from this order, then let Value and Usage argue with it.
Value
How many more points he scores than the best player at his position you could still pick up for free, once every team's starting slots are full. It is the size of the gap he puts between you and the next man up. Jahmyr Gibbs shows 1 while his Rank is 18: seventeen players score more, but none opens a bigger gap. When two players look close on Rank, take the better Value.
Usage
A second opinion built only from the work he gets — carries, targets, and the share of touches left behind by team-mates who have gone. It never changes the order; it argues with it. Christian McCaffrey shows 10 against a Rank of 35: the workload says top ten even though the points projection does not. A small triangle marks the players where the two disagree most.
vs ESPN
How far this board’s order departs from the order ESPN serves the room, compared only against players at the same position. Positive means this board takes him earlier than the room does — he is a bargain. Mike Evans shows +20: twenty receivers the room takes ahead of him, this board takes behind him. Green is a bargain, red means the room likes him more, and anything within ±2 stays grey because that is noise. The same tint is on the Value number, because that is the number it argues about.
Lasts
The chance he is still unpicked when your next turn comes round. Derrick Henry shows 93% — he will very likely still be there. Jahmyr Gibbs shows 9% — he will very likely be gone. Spend this pick on a low number and come back for a high one.

Signals — one chip per note beside each player

green argues for the player   red argues against   grey is context that argues neither way   +2 means two more not shown — hover the cell for all of them.

opp+80
The workload model puts him 80 points above the points projection. It only appears when the two disagree by more than 20 points. Treat it as a reason to buy.
opp-28
The workload model puts him 28 points below the points projection — he is projected on reputation more than on touches. Treat it as a reason to fade.
riser
His role was growing through last season — finishing the year with more of the work than he started it with.
faller
His role was shrinking through last season.
vac+15%
15% of his team's touches are unclaimed — carries or targets left behind by players who have left. Somebody inherits them, and he is in line.
rookie:offense
His first NFL season, on offence. (rookie:idp is a defender, rookie:special a kicker.) Rookies are projected on consensus alone.
R1.008
Where the NFL drafted him: round 1, pick 8. Earlier picks get more chances to earn a role. Only shown for rookies.
need
He fills a starting slot you have not filled yet.
Triangle ▲
Beside a Usage number: Usage and Rank disagree sharply about this player.
